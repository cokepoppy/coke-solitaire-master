Keep an evidence ledger with the video URL, capture date, app version, duration, frame rate, and source hash. Scan the clip to mark state changes, then extract short transition windows at source rate. The reproducible workflow in [docs/video-frame-audit.md](video-frame-audit.md) preserves encoded PTS through seek windows, writes SHA-256 hashes for decoded RGB pixels, makes aspect-preserving contact sheets, retains selected full-resolution frames and adjacent frames, and independently verifies every retained PTS. Label the top counters, stock/waste, each foundation, all seven tableau columns, controls, modal surfaces, and background separately. Record the timing of deal, flip, draw, move, hint, undo, recycle, and win animations.

## Rules and state model

The game engine keeps stock, waste, four foundations, seven tableau piles, score, elapsed time, move count, options, a bounded undo history, and one Magic charge per deal. A card can move to a tableau pile only in descending rank with alternating color; an empty pile accepts a King. Foundations build upward by suit from Ace to King. Drawing uses the selected one-card or three-card mode, and the waste returns to the stock when it is empty. Face-up sequences move together, and uncovering a hidden tableau card flips it face up. Daily deals are deterministic by local calendar date; the calendar can start today's or a previous date's deal, marks completed dates, and tracks monthly completion milestones. Future dates stay locked.

## Audio and animation status

The original game's audio could not be isolated from the mixed promo and gameplay recordings. The gameplay envelope audit identifies review timestamps, but none is yet verified by listening to a clean action or comparing captured PCM against a standalone original cue. This build synthesizes card-like filtered-noise taps, draws, flips, and moves; short chimes for foundations and wins; and a quiet chord-pad ambient loop. Audio starts after a user gesture, queues cues during a pending `AudioContext.resume()`, and keeps sound effects and music on separate gain controls. Mobile Chrome E2E verifies cue scheduling and setting changes; it does not certify PCM capture or physical-speaker playback. The synthesis is a working approximation, not a claim of matching the original recording or mix.

The interface animates the initial deal, stock-to-waste draw, card landing, drag preview, win treatment, and sound cues. In the 60 fps gameplay capture, the first stock-draw frame appears at source time 23.2167 s with the 6♥ already face up; it reaches the waste position by 23.35 s, an eight-frame 133.333 ms flight. The mobile replica now uses a 133.333 ms linear stock-to-waste flight and displays the face immediately. The first 28 cards leave the stock in Klondike deal order with 40 ms staggering; the seven exposed cards flip face up after traveling. A one- or three-card stock draw travels to the waste and fans left-to-right; the left-hand layout keeps that fan clear of the foundations. Winning displays score, time, moves, and a best-score marker; daily wins are recorded in the calendar. Browser E2E checks card count, origin, timing, fan positions, calendar navigation, completion tracking, and the result flow. The default face style uses locally cached Solitaire Master source captures and documented source composites; the optional Classic face style and red/blue backs use CC0 art credited in [assets/cards/README.md](../assets/cards/README.md). Exact proprietary backgrounds, transition curves, and soundtrack still need clean original assets or source-quality captures for an exact match.
