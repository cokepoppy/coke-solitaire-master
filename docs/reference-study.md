# Reference study

## Sources and observed scope

- The supplied Hungry Studio product-matrix image identifies `Solitaire Master!` as its classic card game.
- The official [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=solitaire.classic.hungrystudio.free.klondike.card.patience) describes a 28-card, seven-column Klondike deal, four Ace-to-King foundations, descending alternating-color tableau stacks, King-only empty columns, stock recycling, hints, undo, themes, left-hand mode, and statistics.
- I opened the six official Google Play screenshots in Chrome at 1080×1920 and inspected their pixels. They show bright green felt, a hamburger at top left, compact score/time/moves icons, four empty Ace foundation frames at left, waste near the right, stock at far right, and New Game / Magic / Hint / Undo controls along the bottom. The face cards use illustrated court art; the captured card backs include red ornamental and blue diamond patterns. A mountain landscape is an alternate background.
- The [Hungry Studio YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@hungrystudio_games) has an official short titled [“solitaire classic”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yvi1HA3OcxU). I played it in Chrome and captured all presented frames with `requestVideoFrameCallback`: 11.041 seconds, 640×360, 30 fps, 330 callbacks covering media times 0.000–11.000 seconds. It has a logo intro, a deal animation around 1.2–2.6 seconds, visible play and counter/card changes around 3–9.6 seconds, then a title outro from about 10.5 seconds. Promo text is burned into the clip, so it helps establish visible states and relative animation timing but does not provide clean pixels for exact comparison.
- I also captured the official short's audio track in Chrome with `captureStream()` and sampled it through Web Audio. It exposes one mixed track, with loud RMS peaks near 3.09, 3.59, 4.04, and 5.11 seconds. These are media-time markers, not attributed game SFX; the short does not separate promotional music from card sounds. The capture details and limits are in [docs/audio-audit.md](audio-audit.md).
- I sampled a third-party [Solitaire Master gameplay recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gLd0HXtDt5I) at selected timestamps. It showed onboarding and live gameplay, but later stalled and then displayed a player error; it was not a complete frame audit. The original soundtrack and isolated sound effects remain unavailable for direct comparison.

## Frame-by-frame workflow

Keep an evidence ledger with the video URL, capture date, app version, duration, frame rate, and source hash. Scan the clip to mark state changes, then extract short transition windows at source rate. Preserve original presentation timestamps with `ffmpeg -copyts`; do not rely on seek-rebased timestamps. Record decoded frame count and PTS, make contact sheets for each window, and keep full-resolution captures for key transitions. Label the top counters, stock/waste, each foundation, all seven tableau columns, controls, modal surfaces, and background separately. Record the timing of deal, flip, draw, move, hint, undo, recycle, and win animations.

The official short was frame-scanned through browser presentation callbacks; exact encoded PTS and a stable source hash were not preserved. This is a visual/timing audit, not a bit-exact frame archive. The synchronized replica notes in the user's memory folder describe a more rigorous `ffmpeg -copyts` workflow and hash checks for future clean source footage.

For audio, first obtain a clean gameplay recording with music and game effects audible, then keep a timestamped action log aligned to video. Extract audio without rebasing its source timeline, listen to short event windows, and compare duration, pitch contour, attack/decay, noise texture, stereo position, and music loop period. Keep one sample per event (tap, draw, flip, move, invalid drop, foundation, win) and compare browser PCM capture separately from the source. A successful `AudioContext.resume()`, cue scheduling, PCM output, and audible phone-speaker playback are separate checks; passing the first two alone does not prove the last two.

## Rules and state model

The game engine keeps stock, waste, four foundations, seven tableau piles, score, elapsed time, move count, options, a bounded undo history, and one Magic charge per deal. A card can move to a tableau pile only in descending rank with alternating color; an empty pile accepts a King. Foundations build upward by suit from Ace to King. Drawing uses the selected one-card or three-card mode, and the waste returns to the stock when it is empty. Face-up sequences move together, and uncovering a hidden tableau card flips it face up. Daily deals are deterministic by local calendar date; the calendar can start today's or a previous date's deal, marks completed dates, and tracks monthly completion milestones. Future dates stay locked.

## Audio and animation status

The original game's audio could not be isolated from the mixed promotional video track. This build synthesizes card-like filtered-noise taps, draws, flips, and moves; short chimes for foundations and wins; and a quiet chord-pad ambient loop. Audio starts after a user gesture, queues cues during a pending `AudioContext.resume()`, and keeps sound effects and music on separate gain controls. Mobile Chrome E2E verifies cue scheduling and setting changes; it does not certify PCM capture or physical-speaker playback. The synthesis is a working approximation, not a claim of matching the original recording or mix.

The interface animates the initial deal, stock-to-waste draw and flip, card landing, drag preview, win treatment, and sound cues. The first 28 cards leave the stock in Klondike deal order with 40 ms staggering; the seven exposed cards flip face up after traveling. A one- or three-card stock draw travels to the waste, reveals its face, and fans left-to-right; the left-hand layout keeps that fan clear of the foundations. Winning displays score, time, moves, and a best-score marker; daily wins are recorded in the calendar. Browser E2E checks card count, origin, timing, fan positions, calendar navigation, completion tracking, and the result flow. The board uses locally cached CC0 illustrated cards and red/blue backs, credited in [assets/cards/README.md](../assets/cards/README.md), and its layout follows the official store screenshots. Exact proprietary backgrounds, card variations, transition curves, and soundtrack still need clean original assets or source-quality captures for an exact match.
